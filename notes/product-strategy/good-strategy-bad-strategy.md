## The Three Parts of Good Strategy

Good strategy has three parts:

1. **Diagnosis** — a clear-eyed assessment of the challenge. What's actually going on? What's the critical obstacle?

2. **Guiding policy** — an overall approach for dealing with that challenge. Not a goal, but a governing constraint or direction.

3. **Coherent actions** — specific, coordinated steps that follow from the policy. Everything should reinforce everything else.

> **Why this matters:** A framework to distinguish real strategy (with diagnosis, policy, and coherent action) from mere goal-setting.

## What Makes Strategy "Bad"

* **Fluff** — vague, buzzword-laden language that sounds strategic but says nothing
* **Failure to face the challenge** — avoiding the hard diagnosis and just stating objectives instead
* **Mistaking goals for strategy** — "we will grow revenue 20%" is not a strategy
* **Bad strategic objectives** — goals that are either impossible, irrelevant, or don't address the real problem

> **Why this matters:** Know the common failure modes: fluff, denial, goal-chasing, and misaligned objectives. Clarity means naming the real constraint.

## Leverage Points

Good strategy involves identifying a leverage point — a place where focused effort can produce an outsized result. This requires intellectual honesty about your situation, your strengths, and the competitive or organisational landscape. It also requires saying no to lots of things, which is why genuinely good strategy is uncomfortable and politically difficult.

> **Why this matters:** Strategy is about finding asymmetry — where your effort compounds. Requires honesty and the courage to say no.

---

> **Why this matters (overall):** These three ideas build on each other — the three-part structure (diagnosis, policy, coherent action) is what "good" strategy actually is, the failure modes are the ways people skip that structure and call the substitute "strategy" anyway, and leverage points are what a genuinely good diagnosis should be hunting for.
