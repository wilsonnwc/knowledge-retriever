**Feature analytics matter as much as user analytics.** Track not just whether users adopt a feature, but whether they use it as expected.

**Core set of analytics to track:**

1. **User behavior analytics** — click paths, engagement
2. **Business analytics** — active users, conversion rate, lifetime value, retention
3. **Financial analytics** — average selling price (ASP), billings, time to close
4. **Performance** — load time, uptime
5. **Operational costs** — storage, hosting
6. **Go-to-market costs** — acquisition costs, cost of sales, programs
7. **Sentiment** — NPS, customer satisfaction, surveys

**Critical limitation:** Data will shine a light on WHAT's happening, but only further qualitative techniques can explain WHY. Numbers show the gap; interviews explain the cause.

**Privacy:** Always anonymise and aggregate data so there's nothing personally identifiable.

> **Why this matters:** A complete analytics stack spans seven categories. But remember: data is evidence of what happened, not explanation of why. Combine quantitative and qualitative.
