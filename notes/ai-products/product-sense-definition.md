The most widely cited definition of product sense comes from Jules Walter in Lenny's Newsletter from 2022:

Product sense is the skill of consistently being able to craft products (or make changes to existing products) that have the intended impact on their users. Product sense relies on (1) empathy to discover meaningful user needs and (2) creativity to come up with solutions that effectively address those needs.

Walter and many others, such as Jackie Bavaro, Julie Zhuo, and Marty Cagan, have tried to provide advice for building product sense; most of it revolves around simply consuming product-related inputs.

Author proposed:
Product sense is the ability to recognize when current problems match past successes or failures and reliably estimate how similar solutions will affect the desired outcomes…
Building real product sense requires people to close the full experimentation loop:
- Face problems
- Choose solutions
- Measure the outcomes
- Reflect on the results

Practical suggestions:
* Stay on a project long enough to see the outcome before moving on.
* Write down your hypothesis and expected outcome before you see results (avoids hindsight bias).
* Measure actual outcomes, not just whether you shipped something.
* Reflect explicitly afterwards: what held up, what didn't, why, and what past experience this most resembles.
